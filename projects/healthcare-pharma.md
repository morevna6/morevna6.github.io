<section class="project-section">

  <h2>Project Overview</h2>

  <p>
    This project investigates how scientific activity, disease burden,
    investment, regulatory approvals, and commercial performance interact
    across the global healthcare and pharmaceutical landscape.
  </p>

  <p>
    Rather than analysing a single dataset in isolation, the study combines
    several related datasets to build a wider view of healthcare innovation.
    The objective was to identify where research activity and commercial
    resources align with global health needs, and where meaningful gaps remain.
  </p>

</section>

<section class="project-section">

  <h2>Business and Research Problem</h2>

  <p>
    Healthcare innovation cannot be evaluated through one metric alone.
    A therapy area may attract substantial funding but generate relatively
    few approvals. Another may carry a high disease burden while receiving
    limited commercial or research attention.
  </p>

  <p>
    The project was designed around the following central question:
  </p>

  <blockquote>
    How effectively are research activity, investment, regulatory approvals,
    and commercial resources distributed relative to global disease burden?
  </blockquote>

</section>

<section class="project-section">

  <h2>Innovation Gap Framework</h2>

  <p>
    A central component of the project is an innovation-gap framework
    designed to compare global health need with the level of attention
    received from the pharmaceutical and biotechnology ecosystem.
  </p>

  <p>
    The framework combines several ranked dimensions:
  </p>

  <ul class="project-list">
    <li>Disease burden rank</li>
    <li>Drug approval rank</li>
    <li>Commercial output rank</li>
    <li>Clinical trial activity rank</li>
    <li>Combined innovation rank</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    The innovation-gap score compares burden with innovation activity.
    A positive score indicates that a therapy area may be receiving less
    attention relative to its measured burden, while a negative score may
    indicate comparatively stronger innovation activity.
  </p>

  <div class="analysis-note">
    <strong>Interpretation note:</strong>
    The framework is comparative rather than causal. It identifies patterns
    for further investigation, not definitive evidence that a therapy area
    is objectively overfunded or underfunded.
  </div>

</section>

<section class="project-section">

  <h2>Limitations</h2>

  <ul class="project-list">
    <li>
      The datasets represent analytical samples and do not cover every
      global clinical trial, approval, company, or funding event.
    </li>

    <li>
      Disease burden, funding, approvals, and financial outcomes are
      measured using different units and cannot be compared directly
      without normalisation or ranking.
    </li>

    <li>
      Correlation between variables does not establish causation.
    </li>

    <li>
      Therapy-area classifications may simplify complex medical categories.
    </li>

    <li>
      The innovation-gap score is a comparative analytical construct,
      not a clinical or policy recommendation.
    </li>

  </ul>

</section>
